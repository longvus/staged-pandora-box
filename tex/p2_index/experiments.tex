\subsection{Independent-arm verification}
\label{app:finite-results}

Two independent binary-tree arms are generated in four collection regimes:
every stage on depth three, terminal only on depth four, $\{1,3\}$ on depth
three, and $\{2,4\}$ on depth four. At each own history, independently choose
a cost from $\{1/100,3/100,3/50,1/10\}$, two distinct readings from
$\{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1\}$, and a branch probability from $\{1/4,1/2,3/4\}$.
Each regime uses seeds $2026102400$ through $2026102423$ and is evaluated from
both the empty state and a prefix after three random feasible advances,
giving 192 starting instances. Prefixes are chosen without an index or
stopping criterion and retain their observed incumbent.

The covered-call value, maximal-index actions above the incumbent, and all
stop and advance losses agree with direct Bellman optimization over
$23{,}839$ evaluated states and $37{,}467$ available advance actions.

\subsection{Staging and policy-loss verification}
\label{app:staging-results}

Each independent arm reveals two noisy signals followed by a fair binary
terminal reward $H_i$. Conditional on $H_i$, signals $S_{i1},S_{i2}$ are
independent and equal $H_i$ with probability $\alpha$. The readings are
$(2S_{i1}/5,3S_{i2}/5,H_i)$, and predictions condition on both signals.
The grid crosses
\[
\begin{split}
 \alpha&\in\{1/2,5/8,3/4,7/8,1\},\qquad
 k\in\{1/200,1/50,1/20,1/10\},\\
 c&\in\{1/20,1/5,2/5,3/5,4/5\},\qquad
 \cK_i\in\{\{3\},\{1,3\},\{1,2,3\}\}.
\end{split}
\]
With arm multipliers $m_i\in\{1,5/4\}$, the constant-cost family uses
$m_i(k,k,c)$ and the own-history-cost family uses
\[
 m_i\left(k,\;k(1+S_{i1}/2),\;
 c\left(1+\tfrac12\mathbf 1\{S_{i1}\ne S_{i2}\}\right)\right).
\]
Each next cost is known before the advance. Deterministic controls have readings
$(0,1/2,1)$ and $(0,3/8,3/4)$, costs $m_i(k,k,c)$, and the same cost and
collection grids. There are 100 constant-cost cells, 100 own-history-cost cells
and 20 deterministic controls per collection regime, giving 660 cells.
All runs start at $q_0=0$; zero-probability prefixes at $\alpha=1$ are omitted.
The index stops on an index--incumbent tie and breaks advance ties by the smaller label.

Three specified deviations check Theorem~\ref{thm:loss}.
\emph{Stop after one} takes the initial index advance and stops, or stops immediately
if the index does. \emph{Lowest index first} forces a smallest-index available arm
once, even if stopping was optimal, then follows the index.
\emph{Extra at stop} follows the index to its first stop, takes one cheapest
available advance if any, and then follows the index. All action ties favor
the smaller label. Each deviation's utility loss equals its accumulated stop
and advance losses in every cell. The index agrees with the Bellman optimum
over $48{,}840$ state evaluations and $74{,}160$ advance-action checks,
including all 300 own-history-cost cells.

\subsection{Comparative statics and resumption}
\label{app:comparative-results}

A dominated transition pairs staging cells with the same path laws and arms,
but the next larger collectable set, next lower signal cost, next lower terminal
cost, or constant in place of own-history costs. Coupling each arm's capped
values verifies Proposition~\ref{prop:comparative} on all $1{,}763$ transitions.
The identity also holds on 60 random binary-tree pairs of depths two to four
(seed $20261030$), with enlarged collection sets and costs multiplied by
$1$, $1/2$ or $3/4$, including unchanged cases.

Table~\ref{tab:comparative} summarizes the three checks by transition type,
collectable set and cost law.
For Proposition~\ref{prop:fewer-rights}, terminal-only indices are used under
intermediate collection in every cell with intermediate rights. The policy
attains the expanded-rights optimum in nearly half of these cells and, in about
a quarter, loses the full value of the additional rights, so both ends of the
proposition's bound are attained. At $\alpha=1/2$, $k=1/200$ and $c=3/5$ with
$\cK_i=\{1,3\}$, for instance, the terminal-only indices are negative, the
policy stops at once, and its loss is the full rights value.
For Proposition~\ref{prop:resumption}, a separate Bellman recursion forbids
further advances of any arm once another arm is advanced after it, while
retaining all collectable readings. This gives $V_{\rm NR}$.
Resumption has positive value exactly in the stochastic cells where the index
policy resumes, and in no deterministic control; the largest gap is under one
twentieth of its cell's optimal value. The bound $\rho$ never exceeds the gap,
is positive in the same cells, and attains the gap in about a fifth of them.

\subsection{Shared-law verification}
\label{app:sharing-results}

The audit has 432 two-stage binary-latent cells, 12 arbitrary three-stage joint
laws with own-history costs, and nine settings of the constructed example in
Appendix~\ref{app:sharing-controls}. All start with unobserved arms and $q_0=0$.
The joint Bellman solver retains dependence; the index recomputes from the true
posterior arm marginals after every observation.

\paragraph{Binary-latent grid.}
Conditional on $\Theta\in\{0,1\}$, complete arm paths are independent.
Arm $i$ has $R_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(a_{i\Theta})$ and first signal
$S_i$ satisfying $\Prob(S_i=R_i\mid R_i,\Theta)=r_i$. Its readings are
$(s_iS_i,R_i)$, with $(s_A,s_B)=(2/5,3/5)$. The terminal is collectable;
each first stage can independently be designated collectable, giving four choices.
Seed $20261026$ generates 12 base models with prior $p_0\in\{1/5,1/2,4/5\}$.
For each arm choose two distinct component probabilities from
$\{1/10,1/5,3/10,1/2,7/10,4/5,9/10\}$, $r_i\in\{3/5,3/4,9/10\}$,
first cost in $\{1/100,1/25,2/25\}$ and terminal cost in $\{1/20,1/5,2/5\}$.
For each model set
\[
\begin{gathered}
 m_i=(1-p_0)a_{i0}+p_0a_{i1},\qquad
 a_{i\theta}(\lambda)=m_i+\lambda(a_{i\theta}-m_i),\\
 \lambda\in\{0,1/100,1/50,1/20,1/10,1/5,2/5,3/5,1\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
This holds the full initial arm marginal laws, costs and collection choices
fixed within each coupling sweep. At $\lambda=0$ the arms are independent,
giving 48 independent controls among the $12\times9\times4=432$ cells.

\paragraph{Arbitrary three-stage laws.}
Continuing the same seeded generator, twelve models assign normalized positive
integer weights from $\{1,\ldots,9\}$ to all 64 binary joint paths of two
three-stage arms. Base costs are $(1/20,1/10,1/5)$; the second cost is $1/4$
after first reading one, and the terminal cost is $1/25$ after prefix $(0,1)$.
For model $b\in\{0,\ldots,11\}$ and arm $i\in\{0,1\}$, stage one is
collectable when $(b+i)\bmod2\ne0$, stage two when $(b+i)\bmod3\ne0$, and
stage three always. These laws test sharing without assuming the binary-latent factorization.

\paragraph{Checks and results.}
Every feasible prefix and available advance is checked, including histories
the index never visits. The index stops on an index--incumbent tie and breaks
advance ties by smaller label. The Bellman policy used for the refined bound
favors advances over stopping, then smaller label; this fixes its occupancy at
a value tie. Policy telescoping is checked for the index, the true optimum,
immediate stopping, and a smallest-label always-advance policy.
Uniform envelopes maximize over all feasible histories; the refined bound
uses optimal-policy occupancy and is an oracle diagnostic.
Across the 453 cells, the signed residual, telescoping and call/TV inequalities
hold over $24{,}309$ feasible prefixes and $21{,}042$ available advances.
The independent controls have zero sharing envelopes and zero regret.
Negative signed corrections occur, supporting the two-sided accounting
in~\eqref{eq:sharing-refined}. Counts measure verification coverage;
these known-law checks do not assess learned-surrogate accuracy or practical
optimization performance.

\subsection{Positive regret, shared nuisance and concentration}
\label{app:sharing-controls}

\paragraph{Positive-regret example.}
Use the binary-latent construction above with $p_0=4/5$,
$(a_{A0},a_{A1})=(4/5,1/5)$, $(a_{B0},a_{B1})=(7/10,1/5)$,
$r_A=r_B=3/4$, first scores $(2/5,3/5)$, first costs $(19/500,1/20)$,
and terminal costs $(1/5,1/5)$. Both stages are collectable, $q_0=0$,
and the same nine coupling settings are included in the audit.
Both initial indices are $1/2$; ties favor $A$.
The positive regret in Table~\ref{tab:robustness-bounds} demonstrates the loss
of exact optimality under sharing, while each displayed bound covers that regret.

\paragraph{Shared nuisance.}
Outside the 453-cell audit, let the paths be $(U,R_A)$ and $(U,R_B)$, where
$U$ is a shared fair bit and $R_A,R_B$ are independent
$\operatorname{Bernoulli}(4/5)$ rewards, also independent of $U$.
First readings are noncollectable at cost $1/100$; terminals are collectable
at cost $1/5$. The shared bit changes predictions of another noncollectable
reading but leaves local continuation values unchanged.
The call envelope is zero, the index is optimal with utility $177/250$, and
the uniform path-TV bound is $28/5$. Root joint/product TV is $1/2$, a different
quantity from the posterior marginal shifts in the path-TV envelope.
The control isolates why local-value changes, rather than all remaining-path
changes, are the discrepancy relevant to the bound.

\paragraph{Concentration sweep.}
Also outside the audit, the 12 base models are reused at full component
contrast ($\lambda=1$) with all four collection choices, and only the prior
$p=\Prob(\Theta=1)$ is varied over $\{1/2,1/4,1/10,1/40,1/160\}$: 240 cells
in which the belief about the binary component concentrates while the
component laws, reliabilities, costs and collection stay fixed, each running
the same exact audit as the grid. For this binary component, $\eta_i$ at the
root is $2p(1-p)$ times the component separation of the next reading
(Corollary~\ref{cor:component}). Table~\ref{tab:concentration} reports, for
each prior, the largest regret and the largest uniform call and path-TV bounds
over its 48 cells. Among the tested priors, regret is positive only at $p=1/2$,
and the largest uniform bounds fall by more than an order of magnitude from
$p=1/2$ to $p=1/160$. The bounds remain conservative. Their decrease is an
observed property of this prior-sensitivity calculation on fixed finite laws;
the corollary requires control of dispersion over all feasible future histories,
in addition to concentration at the root.
