\section{Numerical Verification}
\label{sec:validation}

Exact finite-law checks in rational arithmetic compare the representation, losses and bounds
with direct joint Bellman optimization (Table~\ref{tab:verification-summary};
Appendix~\ref{app:experiments} gives each study's law, checks and results).

\paragraph{Independent optimality and action losses.}
On independent binary-tree instances, the optimal value, maximal-index actions and every
stop/advance loss agree with the Bellman solution (Theorems~\ref{thm:index}
and~\ref{thm:loss}). On the staging grid, each diagnostic policy's utility loss equals its
accumulated action losses.

\paragraph{Comparative statics.}
Across dominated problem pairs, value differences equal the covered-call overshoot
(Proposition~\ref{prop:comparative}); both ends of the smaller-collectable-set bound are
attained (Proposition~\ref{prop:fewer-rights}). A separate no-return recursion confirms that
$\rho$ never exceeds the resumption gap, including cases of equality
(Proposition~\ref{prop:resumption}, Table~\ref{tab:comparative}).

\paragraph{Sharing identities and regret bounds.}
Under shared laws, the signed residual, policy telescoping and envelope bounds of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharing-robustness} and Corollary~\ref{cor:component} hold at every
feasible prefix, including histories the index never visits. A constructed example has
positive index regret, covered by each bound, while a shared nuisance has zero local-value
envelope despite nonzero path-TV shifts (Table~\ref{tab:robustness-bounds}). Across the
tested priors in a binary-component sweep, the largest uniform call and path-TV bounds
decrease as the prior moves toward zero, and regret is zero at the four tested priors below
$1/2$ (Table~\ref{tab:concentration}), illustrating the uniform-envelope condition of
Corollary~\ref{cor:component} for these fixed finite models. The checks supplement the
proofs under known laws; they establish neither learned-surrogate accuracy nor practical
optimization performance.
